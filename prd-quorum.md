# PRD: Quorum — HOA / Co-op Governance with a Verifiable Tally

**Sample business:** "Larkspur Commons," a 120-unit condominium association (synthetic): owners, units with declaration shares in basis points, a board, one annual meeting with two elections and four resolutions, proxies, a dues ledger and a violation workflow
**Build log:** #21 HOA / Co-op Governance, main track. Chips: ballots · quorum · weighted votes · verifiable tally
**Status:** Draft v1.0 — kickoff kit, oracle run and traps verified (see `docs/decisions.md` §Review log)
**Learning objectives:** elections with ballots, quorum thresholds, weighted votes by unit share, proxies without chains, resolution thresholds as data, abstentions done right, ties left unbroken, and the trust problem nothing else in the portfolio touches: a secret ballot whose tally anyone can verify. Plus a small dues ledger and a violation state machine so the association is a whole business, not a voting widget.

**Positioning note:** the roster, roles and audit are foundation work; the dues ledger and violation workflow reuse LoanBook's and Adjuster's append-only patterns. The lesson is the tally engine (`src/tally/`) and the sealed-link schema around it. `docs/portfolio-overlap.md` lists what is ported, from where, and what is new.

`docs/decisions.md` outranks this document wherever they differ.

## ⚠️ Scope Honesty (read first)

Learning project, synthetic data only. Every owner, unit, share, proxy, ballot and ledger entry is generated by `tools/build_fixtures.py` from a seed; emails use `example.com`; the association, its bylaws and its candidates are invented. No real association's declaration, bylaws or state statute is modeled: the rules vocabulary (`docs/domain-brief.md` §7) is a design, not legal advice, and a real association must map its own documents onto it. The ballot scheme gives **individual verifiability** (a voter can confirm their ballot is in the published commitment) and **tally verifiability** (anyone can recount the published anonymized ballots and recompute the commitment). It does **not** give **coercion resistance** or **receipt-freeness**: a voter who shows their receipt and nonce proves how they voted. Those are named non-goals. Nothing here is a certified election system. Screens and write-ups report what was measured against the oracle, never "secure" or "accurate" without the number. Banner verbatim in the README.

## Problem Statement

An association's annual meeting decides who sits on the board and whether 120 households pay a $180,000 roof assessment. The meeting is run by volunteers with a sign-in sheet, a stack of proxy forms, a spreadsheet of who owns what, and a bylaws clause nobody has read in years that says quorum is "a majority of units" and the assessment needs "two-thirds of the total ownership interest." The count happens once, in a room, and the losing side has to take the result on faith. Real disputes are about exactly the things the spreadsheet gets wrong: whether the proxy from 4B counts when 4B's owner also showed up; whether the delinquent unit on the third floor could vote; whether 43 yes against 34 no "passes" when the bylaws count all 120 units; whether an abstention is a no; what happens when two committee candidates tie.

Quorum treats the meeting as a tally with a rule for every one of those questions, written as data, run by a pure engine that gives the same answer in any order, and published in a form that lets every owner check two things: that their own ballot was counted as cast, and that the totals follow from the published ballots. The builder lesson: voting integrity is not a cryptographic flourish. It is eligibility as of a date, a closed set of reject codes with a stated precedence, thresholds you can read, ties you refuse to break, and a commitment you can recompute.

## Goals

1. **Every ballot gets exactly one of a closed set of outcomes, with its reason.** `COUNTED`, or one of fifteen reject codes in a stated precedence; the voter sees which, and so does anyone reading the published list.
2. **The tally is deterministic and pure.** `src/tally/` imports no database, clock, randomness, network or model client; the same ballots in any order give the same totals and the same commitment, proven on the fixture by shuffling.
3. **Individual and tally verifiability**, as defined above, with the limits stated: a receipt the voter can find in the Merkle root; a published anonymized list anyone can recount to the same commitment.
4. **The application cannot link a ballot to a voter after close.** The link lives in one sealed table; a trigger revokes the application role's read at close; a tamper test proves the read fails and the recount still matches.
5. **Quorum, weights, thresholds, abstentions and ties follow the rules JSON, and the eval shows what would have happened under the other rules** (both election modes, all six resolution bases, direct-only quorum) so a board can see which clause decided the outcome.
6. **Dues are integer cents on an append-only ledger; violations are a state machine with an event per transition.** Small, but real.
7. **(Builder goal)** Say what the numbers mean: the fixture proves agreement with the spec on 113 ballots with 26 injected rejects and 16 named traps; it does not prove the spec matches any bylaws.

## Non-Goals

- **Coercion resistance and receipt-freeness.** A voter can prove their vote to a third party. Stated everywhere the receipt is shown.
- **Homomorphic tallying, mix-nets, threshold decryption, voter-side cryptography.** The scheme is hashes a board member can recompute with `sha256sum`.
- **Certified election compliance** (any state HOA statute, UOCAVA-style rules, accessibility certification). Design only.
- **Real bylaws.** Rules JSON is the only source (O-9).
- **Proxy chains**, cumulative voting (O-4), write-ins, nominations, candidate eligibility, minutes, e-signatures on proxies.
- **Payment collection, liens, foreclosure, collections letters.** The ledger records; nothing is collected.
- **Architectural review, reserve studies, management-company multi-tenancy.** Seams only.
- **Sending anything.** Meeting and violation notices go to the outbox and stop there.

## Personas

- **Nadia, board secretary** — runs the meeting. Opens and closes the ballot window, accepts proxy forms, needs to know quorum the moment it is reached and whether it is reached without the proxies, and reads the result with the clause that decided it.
- **Soren, owner of a 1BR** — voted no on the assessment and wants to know it counted. Needs his receipt, the published list, and a one-line way to confirm his receipt is in the root.
- **Keiko, treasurer** — posts dues and the fine from last month's hearing, needs the delinquent list as of the record date to be the one the engine used, and needs a passed assessment to post to every unit by share to the cent.
- **Gavin, manager** — walks the property, sends violation notices, schedules hearings, records outcomes; needs an illegal transition refused, not a free-text status.

## Requirements — Must-Have (P0)

**P0-1: Roster — units, shares, owners, ownership as of a date**
- [ ] `Unit` (id, type, building, floor), `UnitShare` (bps, `effectiveFrom`) with the sum-to-10000 check per as-of date (D-9), `Owner`, `Ownership` over `[from, to)` (E-1); three owners with two units; co-owned units; one mid-year sale
- [ ] Share and owners as of the record date reproduce GQ-01, GQ-02 and the fixture (A-301 = 60, B-610 = 151, A-407 new owner)

**P0-2: Tally engine, pure** *(core learning artifact #1)*
- [ ] `src/tally/`: evaluate ballots (B-1..E-8), selections (S-1..S-5), quorum (Q-1..Q-4), resolutions (T-1..T-6), elections (T-7..T-11), receipts and commitments (C-1..C-5); no forbidden import (`tally:lint`)
- [ ] Rules JSON validated against a closed schema; any other `proxy_max_depth`, unknown basis, unknown mode, or shares not summing to `total_share_bps` → `RULES_INVALID`
- [ ] GT-01..GT-21 and GQ-01..GQ-11 pass in TypeScript with the exact values in the brief
- [ ] Order independence: a vitest shuffles `ballots.csv` 20 times and asserts identical totals and meeting commitment

**P0-3: Eligibility, proxies, duplicates** *(core learning artifact #2)*
- [ ] Delinquency from the ledger as of the record date (E-4, E-5), switched by `rules.delinquency_suspends_voting`; the eval prints how many ballots the flag rejected
- [ ] Proxy rules P-1..P-8 including `PROXY_CHAIN` vs `PROXY_HOLDER_MISMATCH` and `PROXY_LATE` for a proxy received after the ballot it supports
- [ ] One unit, one ballot: first valid cast wins, equal instant → lower ballot id; an owner's direct cast supersedes a counted proxy ballot (`PROXY_SUPERSEDED_BY_OWNER`, D-26) while proxy-after-proxy and proxy-after-owner stay `DUPLICATE_UNIT`; both co-owners delinquent → both `INELIGIBLE_DELINQUENT`, not `DUPLICATE_UNIT`

**P0-4: Quorum, thresholds, ties** *(core learning artifact #3)*
- [ ] Quorum in both modes plus direct-only, binding basis from rules; `NO_QUORUM` labels every contest while totals are still computed
- [ ] Six resolution bases as integer inequalities; pass/fail under all six printed beside the binding result; abstention handling per basis
- [ ] Elections in both modes, the other mode informational with `differs`; boundary ties → `TIE_UNRESOLVED` with open-seat count and tied ids; no machine tiebreak anywhere in `src/`

**P0-5: Receipts and verifiable publication** *(core learning artifact #4)*
- [ ] Receipt and Merkle root exactly C-1..C-3 (GT-01..GT-05); contest and meeting commitments C-4, C-5; published list C-6 sorted by receipt with no unit, caster or timestamp, and with each ballot's `bps_as_of` (D-37)
- [ ] `npm run tally:verify -- --receipt <hex>` says whether a receipt is in the published root; `npm run tally:recount -- --published <csv>` recomputes totals and commitments from the published list alone and compares to the published values
- [ ] Every screen and the README that show a receipt carry the one-sentence limit: "proves inclusion; does not hide how you voted from anyone you show it to"

**P0-6: Sealed link and append-only storage (Phase 2)** *(core learning artifact #5)*
- [ ] `Meeting` (rules JSON, `openedAt`, `closedAt`, `sealedAt`, salt), `Contest`, `Candidate`, `Proxy`, `Ballot`, `BallotSecret` per D-20; eligibility and duplicate rules run at cast time and write `status`/`code` on `Ballot`
- [ ] Triggers: UPDATE/DELETE rejected on `Ballot`, `Proxy`, `BallotSecret`, `LedgerEntry`, `ViolationEvent`; at close, `SELECT` on `BallotSecret` revoked from the application role; casts after close rejected by the DB as well as the engine
- [ ] Tamper tests: app-role `SELECT BallotSecret` fails after close; `UPDATE Ballot` fails; the recount from `Ballot` rows equals the Phase 1 engine's commitments on the fixture loaded through the cast path
- [ ] Idempotent close: closing twice writes nothing the second time; the published commitment is stored once

**P0-7: Evaluation** *(the measured claim)*
- [ ] `npm run tally:eval` prints the brief §11 table: meeting received / counted / rejected by code, quorum in both modes and direct-only, per contest counted / voted / blank / void by code, totals in both modes, result, by-basis table, commitment, meeting commitment; diffs against `fixtures/expected_*.csv`; exits non-zero on any difference
- [ ] `npm run fixtures:check` = `verify_fixtures.py && golden_answers.py && git diff --exit-code fixtures/`
- [ ] `docs/EVAL.md` holds the table as measured by TypeScript, the fixture hashes, and the D-24 wording

**P0-8: Dues ledger and violations (Phase 3)**
- [ ] `LedgerEntry` (`CHARGE`, `PAYMENT`, `FINE`, `CREDIT` with `reversesEntryId`), integer cents, append-only; balance and delinquency as of a date are the engine's E-4/E-5 reused, not re-implemented
- [ ] Monthly dues run posts `monthly_budget × bps / 10000` per unit with the integer remainder on a `ROUNDING` line so the month sums exactly; a `PASSED` special assessment posts per unit by share, tagged with the resolution
- [ ] `Violation` with `ViolationEvent` per transition (D-22); illegal transitions refused with `TRANSITION_INVALID`; `FINE_ASSESSED` posts the ledger `FINE` in the same transaction; notices queued to the outbox, never sent

**P0-9: Views (Phase 3)**
- [ ] **Meeting:** rules as read-only data, open/close, live quorum (units, weighted, direct-only), proxy intake with the reject code shown at intake, ballot cast with the receipt and its limit sentence
- [ ] **Results:** per contest totals in both modes, by-basis table, tie state, commitment; the published list as CSV; the verify-a-receipt box
- [ ] **Roster:** units, shares as of a date, owners, delinquent list as of the record date (board/manager only, O-8)
- [ ] **Ledger** per unit with balance as of any date; **Violations** list and record with the event trail

## Phasing

1. **Pure engine + oracle agreement + eval, no database writes.** Fixtures loaded read-only; `src/tally/` written from the brief; GT/GQ goldens; `tally:eval`, `tally:verify`, `tally:recount` as CLIs over the fixture files; shuffle test; `tally:lint`. No Prisma models yet beyond the template's.
2. **Schema, triggers, sealed link, proofs.** Append-only tables, cast path that runs the engine's ballot rules live, `BallotSecret` with the close-time revoke, idempotent close, tamper tests, recount from `Ballot` rows.
3. **UI surface + dues + violations.** Meeting, results, roster, ledger, violations; outbox notices; dues run and assessment posting.

Phase 2 and 3 task lists are written at the gates.

## Risks

- **Spec optimism.** The oracle agrees with itself. Mitigation: D-24 wording; the "not generated" list; O-items for every bylaws-dependent choice; a hand-written meeting in P1.
- **Sealed link leaks through side channels** (cast timestamps, ballot id order, bps on the published row identifying a rare share value). Mitigation: ballot ids are random (D-40: `B-` + 12 hex, minted with `crypto`; only the order of casts within one second is visible to someone who sorts by id); the published list has no timestamps. **The published list carries `bps_as_of` per ballot (D-37, owner decision 2026-10-26)** so the WEIGHTED totals are recountable from it — at the cost that a rare share value partially identifies a unit: in the fixture 151 bps is one of four penthouses, and 60 and 67 bps are one unit each after the 2026-07-01 amendment. The brief, the README and the published-list screen say so. Not solved, stated. The desk path (D-39) records the operator in the sealed link, not the audit log; an audit timestamp next to `Ballot.castAt` can still be joined by someone holding both.
- **Cloud sandbox cannot run Prisma or Playwright.** Mitigation: D-6; CI decides; the session records which commands it could not run.
- **Threshold vocabulary is wrong for a real association.** Mitigation: it is data; adding a basis is a decision plus a golden case, not a code fork.
- **Scope creep into dues and violations.** Mitigation: Phase 3, task list written at the gate, D-21/D-22 fix the shape now.
