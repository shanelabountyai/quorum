### Engine (Phase 1)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| D-7 | **The engine `src/tally/` is pure and deterministic.** No database, clock, randomness, network or model client; `npm run tally:lint` greps for `Date.now`, `Math.random`, `prisma`, `fetch`, `anthropic`, `new Date(`. Ballots are evaluated in `(cast_at, ballot_id)` order whatever order they arrive in, so the same ballot set gives the same totals and the same commitment. `reference_tally.py --shuffle` proves it on the fixtures. | Goal 2; a recount must not depend on storage order |
| D-8 | **Timestamps are fixed-width strings on one named calendar axis.** `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS` for instants, `YYYY-MM-DD` for dates, compared lexically; `rules.timezone_label` names the zone and is never used for arithmetic. No `new Date(string)`, no `@db.Date`, no UTC conversion in the engine. Ballots are accepted while `open_at <= cast_at < close_at`; `cast_at == close_at` is `LATE`. | Bookable's rule; a meeting closes at a wall-clock instant the bylaws name |
| D-9 | **Shares are basis points that sum to exactly 10000 at every as-of date**, versioned by `effective_from`; the share in force is the latest row with `effective_from <= record_date`. A future-dated amendment is ignored. The engine refuses (`RULES_INVALID`) if the shares as of the record date do not sum to `rules.total_share_bps`. | Declarations state percentages; integers make the weighted tally exact and the trap (B-610) decidable |
| D-10 | **One unit, one ballot.** A unit is represented when it has exactly one `COUNTED` ballot. Co-owners share that one ballot; a second valid cast for the same unit is `REJECT:DUPLICATE_UNIT`. **First valid cast wins** (`rules.duplicate_unit_rule = FIRST_VALID_CAST_WINS`), ordered by `cast_at` then `ballot_id`, **with one exception: the owner's own direct cast supersedes a counted proxy ballot (D-26).** | The brief's default, amended by the owner on O-1 |
| D-11 | **Ballot-level reject codes are a closed set with a stated precedence** (brief §4): `UNKNOWN_UNIT` → `EARLY` → `LATE` → proxy checks P-1..P-8 → `NOT_OWNER` → `INELIGIBLE_DELINQUENT` → `DUPLICATE_UNIT` / `PROXY_SUPERSEDED_BY_OWNER` (E-8/E-9). A rejected ballot still receives a receipt and is still a leaf in the Merkle root, with its code published. | A voter whose ballot was refused can see that it was received and why |
| D-12 | **Selection-level codes void one contest on one ballot, never the ballot.** `INVALID_CHOICE` → `UNKNOWN_CANDIDATE` → `DUPLICATE_CANDIDATE` → `OVERVOTE`. A blank or missing selection is `BLANK`: an abstention on a resolution, no vote in an election; the unit is still represented. Unknown contest keys are counted and reported, not fatal. | An overvote on one race must not throw away the budget vote |
| D-13 | **Eligibility as of the record date.** Owner of record: ownership rows cover `[from_date, to_date)`. Delinquency: ledger balance (Σ CHARGE + FINE − Σ PAYMENT over entries dated `<= record_date`) `> 0`; `rules.delinquency_suspends_voting` turns the suspension on or off. A payment dated on the record date counts; one dated the day after does not. | Bylaws name a record date; the ledger, not a flag, is the source |
| D-14 | **Proxies do not chain.** `rules.proxy_max_depth = 1` is the only implemented value; the engine refuses any other (`RULES_INVALID`). A ballot cast via proxy P (unit A → holder B) by someone other than B is `PROXY_CHAIN` when the caster holds a proxy from B, otherwise `PROXY_HOLDER_MISMATCH`. A proxy received at or after close, **or after the ballot it supports**, is `PROXY_LATE`; one revoked at or before the cast is `PROXY_REVOKED`; a holder who is not an owner of record is `PROXY_HOLDER_NOT_MEMBER` (`rules.proxy_holder_must_be_member`). | A chain is where proxy fraud hides; most condo bylaws forbid it |
| D-15 | **Quorum is computed on units represented (direct + proxy), in both modes, every run.** `units_represented × 10000 >= threshold_bps × total_units` and `bps_represented × 10000 >= threshold_bps × total_share_bps`; `rules.quorum.basis` picks which one binds. The report always shows direct-only as well, so "quorum met only through proxies" is visible. No quorum → every contest reports `NO_QUORUM` with its totals still shown. | Q-rules; the proxy trap in the fixture (49 direct of 120, 85 with proxies) |
| D-16 | **Resolution thresholds are a closed vocabulary of six bases** (brief §7), each an integer inequality: `MAJORITY_OF_VOTES_CAST`, `MAJORITY_OF_UNITS_REPRESENTED`, `MAJORITY_OF_ALL_UNITS`, `TWO_THIRDS_OF_VOTES_CAST`, `MAJORITY_OF_WEIGHTED_SHARES_CAST`, `TWO_THIRDS_OF_ALL_WEIGHTED_SHARES`. Majorities are strict (`>`); two-thirds is `>=`. Abstentions count in the denominator of the `_REPRESENTED` and `_ALL_` bases and not in `_VOTES_CAST`. The eval prints pass/fail under every basis next to the binding one. | Bylaws differ; the data says which applies; the traps R2–R4 show why it matters |
| D-17 | **Elections are plurality, top-N, in `UNITS` or `WEIGHTED` mode per contest.** A ballot may select up to `seats` candidates; each selected candidate receives 1 (UNITS) or the unit's bps (WEIGHTED). **A tie at the seat boundary is never broken by the machine:** seats above the tie are filled, the tied seats are `TIE_UNRESOLVED` with the tied candidates listed, and the board runs a runoff. Lexical or random tiebreaks are refused. The other mode is always computed and printed as informational, with a `differs` flag. | A seat decided by candidate-id order is indefensible to the losing owner; E2 in the fixture is the case |
| D-18 | **Receipts and commitments** exactly brief §8: canonical choices JSON (sorted keys, sorted candidate lists, no whitespace); receipt `SHA-256(ballot_id|nonce|canonical)`; Merkle leaves `SHA-256(receipt)` sorted ascending, parent `SHA-256(left_hex + right_hex)`, odd node carried up, empty root `SHA-256("")`; contest commitment `SHA-256(root|contest_id|canonical totals)`; meeting commitment over contest commitments in id order. The published list is `(receipt, ballot_id, status, code, canonical choices)` sorted by receipt, with no unit or voter. | Fixed so TypeScript and Python can agree byte for byte (GT-01..GT-05) |

### Data model (Phase 2, shape fixed now)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| D-19 | **Ballots, proxies, receipts, ledger entries and violation events are append-only**; UPDATE/DELETE rejected by DB triggers (hand-written SQL in clone migrations). A ballot is never edited; a correction is a new cast, which the engine rejects as `DUPLICATE_UNIT` under D-10 unless the first was itself rejected. | Gavel/LoanBook pattern; a tally over mutable rows proves nothing |
| D-20 | **The sealed link.** `BallotSecret(ballot_id, unit_id, cast_by, via_proxy_id)` is written at cast time by a `SECURITY DEFINER` function and is readable by the application role only while the meeting is `OPEN`; at close a trigger revokes `SELECT` from the application role and sets `meeting.sealed_at`. The `Ballot` row keeps only what the engine needs after close: `meeting_id`, `unit_id` **hash** (`SHA-256(meeting_id|unit_id|meeting salt)`), `bps_as_of`, `cast_at`, `choices`, `nonce_hash`, `receipt`. Eligibility and duplicate detection run **at cast time** against the live roster and write the reject code onto the ballot row; after close the engine recounts from `Ballot` alone. A tamper test (Phase 2) asserts that `SELECT` on `BallotSecret` fails for the app role after close and that the recount from `Ballot` equals the Phase 1 engine's result on the fixture. | The card's "hard trust problem": the app that counts cannot look up who cast what |
| D-21 | **Dues ledger is integer cents, append-only**: `CHARGE`, `PAYMENT`, `FINE`, `CREDIT` (reversal with `reversesEntryId` and reason). Monthly dues per unit = `monthly_budget_cents × share_bps / 10000`, integer division with the remainder posted to the association's `ROUNDING` memo line so the month sums to the budget exactly. A passed special assessment posts one `CHARGE` per unit the same way, tagged with the resolution id. | Money rule; the fixture uses $35,000/month = 350 cents per bp so no remainder arises in Phase 1 |
| D-22 | **Violation state machine as data**: `NOTICE_SENT → HEARING_SCHEDULED → (FINE_ASSESSED | DISMISSED)`, plus `NOTICE_SENT → DISMISSED` (cured) and `HEARING_SCHEDULED → CONTINUED → HEARING_SCHEDULED`. Every transition is a `ViolationEvent` row with actor, reason and the notice/hearing dates; `FINE_ASSESSED` posts the `FINE` to the ledger in the same transaction. Illegal transitions are refused with `TRANSITION_INVALID`. | Phase 3 scope, small on purpose |
